## Question and intervention

Does replacing the spatial-amplitude variance prior inverse-Gamma(shape 10, scale 1) with a half-Cauchy(scale 1) prior on the spatial amplitude improve recovery of the actual spatial field? Scale 1 is fixed for all communities on the linear-predictor scale, with prior median 1 and broad tails. This changes both the concentration and scale of the prior; it is not a matched-median test of tail shape alone. The amplitude is the shared residual spatial-coefficient SD, not an extra kernel-amplitude parameter. Retain the occupancy-intercept, environmental, range, trait, detection and continuous-noise priors. Do not infer success from an amplitude estimate nearer 1 alone.

## Implementation and validation

Add experimental `listPriors$sigma_bs_prior` (`inverse_gamma` by default, or `half_cauchy`) and `sigma_bs_scale` (default 1). Retain the inverse-gamma shape 10 and scale 1 unchanged. Record the applied choice in `infos$spatial_sd_prior`. Reject invalid choices/scales and spatial-prior settings without spatial covariates. Keep this option separate from `tau_prior`, which controls continuous observation noise.

Use the auxiliary inverse-gamma representation of the half-Cauchy: variance conditional on auxiliary is IG(1/2, 1/auxiliary); auxiliary is IG(1/2, 1/scale^2). Given current variance, refresh auxiliary from IG(1, 1/variance + 1/scale^2), then draw variance from IG((m+1)/2, residual sum of squares/2 + 1/auxiliary), where m is the number of spatial coefficients across species and residuals subtract observed and latent trait means. Reference: [Makalic and Schmidt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.03884). The collapsed range update and immediate coefficient redraw retain their order. Half-Cauchy fits start amplitude at the prior median (scale 1), avoiding an unnecessary near-zero transient with hundreds of centred coefficients; inverse-gamma starts remain exactly .001. Other starting values and the input RNG state remain unchanged. This is a starting-state change, not a posterior-target change.

Write regression tests before code: direct numerical integration of the conditional SD density, trait-mean removal, unit rescaling, validation, active public fitting and default seeded equivalence.
