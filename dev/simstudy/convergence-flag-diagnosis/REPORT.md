# Worst remaining convergence flag: report

Written 30 September 2026 for Doug and Alex. It closes release item 3 of [TODO.md](../../../TODO.md). Every number below comes from the committed files under [results/](results/), named in brackets, or from the records they cite; no new analysis or fit was made for this report. Occupancy errors are in percentage points unless a probability is stated. Sentences marked *Interpretation* go beyond what a result shows.

## Question and answer

In the current-code comparison for PR #11 ([current-code report](../current-main-recheck/REPORT.md), "Numerical checks and limits"), the high-contamination, 300-site two-stage (eDNA) fit of community 5, `design-qfar_K6-sites300-05`, kept a substantial convergence flag after the longer schedule of 4 chains, each with 6,000 burn-in and 12,000 retained iterations: Rhat up to 1.74, and an effective sample size of about six for the original-site low-probability band. Four other fits kept milder flags. We asked why, and whether the results that rely on these fits can be trusted.

**Answer: for one species under heavy laboratory contamination the data allow two stable labellings of the sites, one close to the truth and one a mirror image of it; the sampler enters the mirror rarely from its default start; with 16 fresh chains the posterior is one near-truth mode, and no earlier conclusion changes.** In the pr11 fit two of the four chains held species 6 in each labelling for all their retained iterations, and pooling them produced the flag. The mirror swaps the roles of real DNA and contamination. All 16 new chains, each with 10,000 burn-in and 40,000 further iterations, stayed in the near-truth labelling, and the 16-chain fit is unflagged under the pr11 rule. With it in place of the pooled fit, the practical conclusions of the current-code comparison stand, and the occupancy-intercept prior study's phase B verdict stays FAIL. The other four flags come from within-chain drift, with the flagged diagnostics of one species per fit, and similar drift appears in unflagged fits of the same design; they need no further runs.

## What was done

- The [plan](PLAN.md) fixed five questions, Q1 to Q5 below, before any new fit. Production code was not changed. The diagnostic fits used a library built from `707540a` in a separate, git-ignored archive, and a short default fit of community 5 with that library was bitwise identical to one with the pr11 library ([equivalence.csv](results/equivalence.csv)).
- Task 1, chain anatomy from saved draws (no fits): per-chain summaries of 13 quantities for every species in the five flagged fits and in three unflagged fits of the same design (`design-qfar_K6-sites300-01`, `-02` and `-03`). Each quantity is labelled *separated* (chains in different places, each stable on its own), *drifting* (some chain not stable on its own) or *agrees* ([results/anatomy/](results/anatomy/)).
- Task 2, community 5 data and truth (no fits): how species 6's positive PCRs arise, set against the true site and sample states, and each group of chains set against the generating values ([results/community5/](results/community5/)).
- Task 3, prespecified diagnostic fits. The protocol was frozen in [README.md](README.md) (commit `183bc2f`) and amended once, still before any fit started ([AMENDMENT-1.md](AMENDMENT-1.md), rulings R13 to R18), chiefly so that each draw is classified against two fixed "anchors" taken from the pr11 chain groups rather than by a mixture refitted to each run, which review showed could misassign an unbalanced split. Then 40 single-chain fits of community 5, each with 10,000 burn-in and 40,000 further iterations thinned by 4 to 10,000 retained draws: an extended run of 16 chains, and 8 chains of each of three variants. Variant (a) uses a tighter Beta(1, 100) prior on every species' field false-positive rate `theta0` instead of the default Beta(1, 20); (b) fixes species 6's `theta0` at its generating value, through a research-only copy of the fitter; (c) removes species 6 from the data. The frozen rules were then applied mechanically ([verdicts.md](results/verdicts.md)).
- Task 4, impact (no fits): the other four flagged fits, the pr11 flag rule applied to the extended run, and every affected comparison rescored with community 5 replaced by each chain group or by the extended run ([impact.md](results/impact.md)).

## Results by question

### Q1. Which parameters disagree between chains, and how?

- In `design-qfar_K6-sites300-05` only species 6 is separated, in four quantities: its field false-positive rate `theta0`, its collection intercept and both environmental occupancy slopes. The gaps between chains are 3.5 to 6.1 pooled within-chain standard deviations, each chain's own split-Rhat is at most 1.008, and the all-chain Rhat is 1.72 to 1.74 ([separation.csv](results/anatomy/separation.csv)).
- Chains 1 and 3 sit in one place and chains 2 and 4 in another for all 12,000 retained iterations, without crossing ([trace figure](results/anatomy/trace-design-qfar_K6-sites300-05.png)); the anchored classifier later built on them puts 1 of their 48,000 draws against its chain's group ([anchor-validation.csv](results/modes/anchor-validation.csv)). Species 6's `B0` and mean occupancy pair the chains the same way but overlap too much to count as separated (separation 0.73 and 0.33). Its `p`, `q` and latent-factor loadings agree across chains.
- In the same fit species 7 drifts within chains (split-Rhat up to 1.064); the other eight species agree ([species-labels.csv](results/anatomy/species-labels.csv)).
- The other four flagged fits have no separated species: their largest separation is 0.505 to 0.77, against a threshold of 3. Their flags come from scored Rhats between 1.05 and 1.075, in each fit all for one species, and from a package warning on `B_output` in two fits; the four-sample fit has only the warning ([other-flagged-fits.csv](results/impact/other-flagged-fits.csv)).
- `design-qnear_K6-sites300-02`: 35 scored diagnostics of species 8 above 1.05 (at most 1.074) and the warning. `design-qfar_K6-sites300-07`: 18 of species 10 (at most 1.055). `design-qfar_K6-sites300-09`: 12 of species 9 (at most 1.054). `design-qfar_K6-field4-09`: the warning only, with no scored diagnostic above 1.05.
- Drift also appears in the three unflagged reference fits, whose largest separations are 0.470 (`-01`), 0.474 (`-02`) and 0.310 (`-03`). In `-02` and `-03` some quantities drift, with a chain's own split-Rhat of 1.055 to 1.098; `-01` has none above 1.05. The clearest drift in the flagged fits is larger (split-Rhat up to 1.275 for species 8 of `design-qnear_K6-sites300-02` and up to 1.175 in `design-qfar_K6-sites300-09`), but it is still drift, not separation.

### Q2. Two modes for species 6: what distinguishes them, and which is near the truth?

In the pr11 fit there are two, each held by two chains ([modes-vs-truth.csv](results/community5/modes-vs-truth.csv)).

- Chains 1 and 3, near-truth: `theta0` 0.036 (truth 0.038), collection intercept -0.66 (truth -0.82), environmental slopes 0.60 and -1.79 (truth 0.70 and -1.87), `B0` -0.49 (truth -0.37); every generating value lies inside its 95% interval. Over the original 100 sites, site occupancy correlates 0.89 with the truth, with a mean absolute error of 0.12 on the probability scale.
- Chains 2 and 4, mirror: `theta0` about 0.25, collection intercept -2.83, and both environmental slopes of the wrong sign (-1.27 and 2.02); the truth lies outside the 95% interval of all four. `B0` is -0.03, and its interval contains the truth. Site occupancy correlates -0.86 with the truth, with a mean absolute error of 0.55. Over all 300 sites, the truly occupied sites get a mean occupancy of 0.27 and the truly unoccupied sites 0.66, against 0.63 and 0.28 in the near-truth chains and 0.73 and 0.25 in truth ([modes-psi-by-true-state.csv](results/community5/modes-psi-by-true-state.csv)).
- Both labellings give the same PCR detection rates `p` and `q` to within 0.016 of the truth, and similar mean occupancy over the original sites (0.475 and 0.448, truth 0.511).
- What the package reports pools the two: `theta0` 0.144, and a site occupancy correlation of -0.25 with the truth.
- *Interpretation:* the mirror treats the truly occupied sites as unoccupied sites whose samples pick up field contamination often (`theta0` about 0.25), and the truly unoccupied sites as occupied sites where DNA is rarely collected: its mean collection probability is 0.061 (95% interval 0.020 to 0.121), against 0.343 in the near-truth chains and 0.31 in truth ([implied-rates.csv](results/community5/implied-rates.csv)). That turns the occupancy surface upside down, which is why both slopes change sign.

### Q3. What in the data allows both explanations?

- Laboratory false positives put a positive PCR at nearly every site, for every species in this design, not only species 6. Each site has 24 PCRs, and a sample without DNA gives a positive PCR with probability 0.13 to 0.24 depending on species and primer, so 98% to 100% of sites have at least one positive for every species. For species 6 it is all 300 sites, including all 173 truly unoccupied ones ([positive-routes.csv](results/community5/positive-routes.csv)). Whether a site has any positive therefore says almost nothing about its occupancy.
- The information is in which samples hold DNA. Only 93 of the 600 field samples hold species 6 DNA: 82 at occupied sites and 11 at unoccupied sites through field contamination. The number of positive PCRs in a sample picks these out well (area under the curve 0.975 for species 6, and 0.85 to 0.97 across the ten species). Of the 890 positives at truly unoccupied sites, 810 come from samples with no DNA. 58 of the 127 truly occupied sites have no DNA-holding sample at all, so they look like unoccupied sites.
- What the data cannot show directly is why a sample holds DNA: because the site is occupied, or through field contamination. Both labellings are consistent with the observed rates. Per retained draw, the implied share of samples holding DNA is 0.166 in the near-truth chains and 0.159 in the mirror chains, against 0.155 observed, and the implied share of positive PCRs 0.256 and 0.254 against 0.255, with the same `p` and `q` ([implied-rates.csv](results/community5/implied-rates.csv)). This is a consistency check, not a likelihood comparison: it does not show that the two labellings fit equally well.
- *Interpretation, untested:* against two species with the same generating collection slope whose chains agree (species 8 and 10), species 6 has fewer DNA-holding samples (93 against 173 and 201) and a smaller share of occupied sites with one (54% against 69% and 76%), so less evidence linking DNA to the site covariates, which may make the mirror cheaper for it. The count alone does not explain it: species 2 has 91 DNA-holding samples and its chains agree.

### Q4. Does the split persist with many more chains and longer sampling?

- No. The frozen verdict for the extended run is **one mode only: near-truth** ([verdict.csv](results/extended/verdict.csv), [16-chain figure](results/extended/chain-strips.png)). All 16 chains stay in the near-truth region: every one of the 160,000 retained draws is labelled near-truth, and none mirror. 1,551 of those near-truth draws (0.97%; 0.57% to 1.37% per chain) are also far from both anchors, below the frozen limit of 5% per chain and similar to the 0.36% to 0.94% of the pr11 chains the anchors came from ([mode-mass.csv](results/extended/mode-mass.csv), [mode-mass-overall.csv](results/extended/mode-mass-overall.csv)). The figure's legend has a key for mirror chains although there are none.
- Both secondary checks agree on every chain: a `theta0` cut at 0.135 puts 177 draws (0.11%) above it, all within near-truth chains, and a refitted mixture finds one mode.
- The largest all-chain Rhat over species 6's quantities is 1.0015 and the largest per-chain split-Rhat 1.0074, and every species agrees across chains ([separation.csv](results/extended/separation.csv)).
- Species 6 in the extended run against the truth: `theta0` 0.036 (0.038), `B0` -0.48 (-0.37), collection intercept -0.66 (-0.82), environmental slopes 0.60 and -1.78 (0.70 and -1.87), mean occupancy over the original sites 0.475 (0.511); every generating value lies inside its 95% interval ([region-means-vs-truth.csv](results/extended/region-means-vs-truth.csv)).
- So there is no sharing of posterior mass between modes to report. The single-mode summary the plan asked about is the extended run's own pooled posterior. The decisive evidence that the mirror is not the converged posterior is that all 16 fresh chains are near-truth.
- Variants ([variants/verdicts.csv](results/variants/verdicts.csv)): all 8 chains of each are near-truth, and because the extended run has no split, the protocol flags all three verdicts as uninformative about it. Variant (a) "explains" (11 of 11 species-6 quantities agree; largest Rhat 1.0019), and (b) "explains" (10 of 10 remaining quantities agree; 1.0015). Variant (c) "isolates": each of the other nine species' mean occupancy changes by less than 0.01 (largest 0.0082, species 1; [occupancy-change.csv](results/variants/c/occupancy-change.csv)). Species 1, 3 and 8 shift by 3 to 5 Monte Carlo standard errors, so their changes are small but real rather than noise; they pass by the 0.01 limit, not by the alternative 2-standard-error condition.
- The mirror did not appear in any chain of variant (a) or (b); variant (c) has no species 6. In variant (a) the tighter prior pulls species 6's `theta0` down to 0.011 and `B0` up to -0.11, with mean occupancy 0.52 (truth 0.038, -0.37 and 0.51); *interpretation:* that is the prior acting within the near-truth region, not a change of mode.
- Why the pr11 fit entered the mirror and the new chains did not is not established. Controller check, a code reading: `runOccJSDM` gives every chain the same deterministic starting values inside its chain loop (`R/runOccJSDM.R` from line 1026). In the pr11 fit the four chains ran one after another in one process, from the saved input random-number state, differing only by their position in one random-number stream, with 6,000 burn-in and 12,000 retained iterations each. The new chains ran as separate processes with distinct seeds, with 10,000 burn-in and 40,000 further iterations. So they differ in more than their number, and which difference matters, if any, was not tested.
- The mirror is real and stable once entered: pr11 chains 2 and 4 stayed in it for all 12,000 retained iterations (their 6,000 burn-in iterations were not saved). But only 2 of the 20 chains run on this model and data, 4 in pr11 and 16 here, entered it.
- *Interpretation:* if the 20 chains were exchangeable, the chance that both mirror chains fall among the 4 pr11 chains would be 6 in 190 (0.032, a one-sided Fisher's exact test). The differences above make exchangeability doubtful, and the test was chosen after seeing the result, so it is not evidence for any particular cause.

### Q5. Do the conclusions that use these fits change?

- The extended run is unflagged under the pr11 flag rule, applied to its 16 chains bound into one fit: 0 fitting warnings, largest group Rhat 1.0040, largest element Rhat 1.0059, and no non-finite Rhat. The same code reproduces the pr11 fit's recorded flag ([flag-rule.csv](results/impact/flag-rule.csv)).
- Community 5 scored on the original 100 sites ([community5-scores.csv](results/impact/community5-scores.csv)): mean absolute error 21.17 as published, with the chains pooled; 19.16 from the near-truth chains; 23.71 from the mirror chains; 19.17 from the extended run.
- Current-code comparison ([affected-comparisons.csv](results/impact/affected-comparisons.csv)): no practical conclusion reverses. With community 5 from the near-truth chains, the mirror chains or the extended run, every affected result that has a published observed-chain sensitivity range stays inside it. Values below are published, then extended run, then that range.
- Overall error at 300 sites under high contamination: 16.97, then 16.77 (near-truth chains 16.77, mirror chains 17.22); range 16.68 to 17.31.
- Improvement from the baseline to 300 sites under high contamination: 3.55, then 3.75, with 10 of 10 communities improving; range 3.21 to 3.84. With the mirror chains alone it is 3.30 and community 5 no longer improves, so "every community improves relative to the baseline" would fail for that estimate only; the mirror chains are not the converged posterior.
- Advantage of 300 sites over four field samples under high contamination: 0.26 (-0.90 to 1.42), then 0.46 (-0.49 to 1.41); range -0.12 to 0.61. The interval spans zero in every case, so there is still no clear winner.
- Signed error at 300 sites under high contamination: below 0.2, +15.34, then +14.89; above 0.8, -18.82, then -18.55. Low probabilities stay too high and high ones too low.
- Current minus archived code, 300 sites, high contamination: 0.249 (-0.197 to 0.695), then 0.048 (-0.138 to 0.235). The interval still includes zero; no observed-chain range was published for this change. The largest two-stage code change quoted in the report becomes 0.226, the published known-site-factor value, instead of 0.249.
- *Interpretation:* the published pooled estimate averaged two chains in each labelling. It overstated community 5's error by about 2.0 points, and the ten-community error of the high-contamination 300-site design by about 0.20. Correcting it slightly favours 300 sites and removes most of the apparent code-version change in that arm, but changes no conclusion.
- Occupancy-intercept prior study, phase B ([intercept-prior-phase-b.csv](results/impact/intercept-prior-phase-b.csv)): the FAIL verdict is unchanged for SD 2, 3 and 5. With the extended run as the control for community 5, the control's flagged fits at high contamination fall from 3 to 2, against 4, 9 and 10 for SD 2, 3 and 5, so criterion 4 still fails for every SD; it also fails at low contamination on its own (control 1, against 3, 7 and 9). It still fails if each SD's own flagged community 5 fit is counted as unflagged too (3, 8 and 9 against 2).
- Criteria 1 to 3 keep their pass or fail result for every SD. One component moves: SD 3's overall error at high contamination becomes 0.68 worse than the control instead of 0.48, over the 0.5 limit, but SD 3 already failed criterion 2 on the middle band. The SD arms' own community 5 fits at high contamination are flagged as well (largest element Rhat 1.54, 1.28 and 1.26) and were not refitted, so criteria 1 to 3 are not a like-for-like converged comparison.

## What it means for users and for the release

- For the release: item 3 is closed and nothing needs to change. No production code, default or published result file changes. Checked for this report, no number in the README's Known limitations changes: its two-stage bias figures are for the 100-site designs, which do not use this fit, and its coverage figures come from the phase B control, whose coverage at high contamination changes by at most 0.003 with the converged control (0.716 to 0.719 in the band above 80%; [intercept-prior-phase-b.csv](results/impact/intercept-prior-phase-b.csv)). The current-code report gains only a one-line pointer to this report.
- For users (*interpretation*): when laboratory false positives put a positive PCR at nearly every site, a species can settle into a mirror labelling with high `theta0`, low collection probability and occupancy slopes of the wrong sign. A chain in it looks stable on its own, so only disagreement between chains reveals it, as a large Rhat on that species' `theta0`, collection intercept and slopes, which the package's convergence warnings already report. The practical check is to compare that species' per-chain means of `theta0` and mean occupancy; if the chains fall into two groups, run more chains rather than reporting the pooled summary, which averages the two labellings. This guidance is listed for the post-beta documentation, not added to the README now.
- How often this happens in practice is unknown: it is one fit of one species in these studies.

## Limitations

- One community, one species and one design. Nothing here says how common mirror labellings are in other data.
- The mechanism of the pr11 entry into the mirror is not established. The pr11 and diagnostic chains differ in process layout, seeding, burn-in and length (Q4), and no fit isolated any of these.
- The variants are uninformative about the split, because the extended run had none to explain or isolate. They show only that each variant stays near-truth from the default start.
- The Fisher calculation assumes the 20 chains are exchangeable, which the differences between the pr11 and diagnostic chains make doubtful, and it is post hoc. It is offered as interpretation only.
- The anchored classifier was calibrated on the same pr11 fit it was checked against (across chains and across halves of chains). A region unlike both anchors would show up as draws far from both, and no chain came near the 5% limit, but the anchors have not been tested on draws from another fit with a known split.
- Counting chains shows where chains go from the default start, not how much posterior mass the mirror holds. No likelihood or posterior-density comparison of the two labellings was made.
- The flagged community 5 fits of the intercept-prior study's SD 2, 3 and 5 arms were not examined for mirror labellings.

## Recommended follow-ups (suggestions only, not approved)

- Estimate how often the mirror is entered: replicate pr11-style fits of community 5, four chains run one after another in one process at the pr11 schedule (6,000 burn-in, 12,000 retained), with new seeds, and count the chains that enter it. Then test whether a longer burn-in or dispersed starting values change that rate.
- Add a user-facing diagnostic for mirror labellings under high contamination, such as a table of per-chain species means of `theta0`, the collection intercept and mean occupancy that marks species whose chains split into groups, with guidance in the fitting documentation.
- Optionally, check whether the flagged SD 2, 3 and 5 fits of community 5 in the intercept-prior study show the same mirror, using `anatomy.R` on their saved draws (no new fits).
