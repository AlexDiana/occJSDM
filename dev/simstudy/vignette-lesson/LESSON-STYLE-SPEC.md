# Lesson rewrite in the teaching style: design

Written 2 October 2026 from the five diagnostic reads in `style-diagnostic/` and Doug's decisions in discussion the same day. This is the spec the implementation plan argues from. Doug reviews each lesson line by line before it is published, so every change here is one he will see in a diff.

## Goal

Rewrite Lessons 0 to 4 so that every section teaches rather than describes, following the criteria in `AGENTS.md` ("Writing the vignettes and lessons"), with the quickstart as guidance, although note that the quickstart, being short, cannot be considered the complete guide for improving pedagogy in these lessons. Keep the verified results, bundles and figures wherever the criteria do not require a change.

## The reader

An ecologist who runs eDNA or presence/absence surveys, knows basic R and the tidyverse pipe, and has not fitted an occupancy model or a JSDM before; which means that it short 'why' explanations for occupancy and JSDM are warranted, although we can assume that these why explanations are more to remind the ecologist than to introduce the concepts of occupancy and SDMs in the first place. A quantitative ecologist should still find the technical evidence, below the point where the first reader can stop.

## The criteria, applied

Each lesson, section by section, must: name the concepts the reader needs in the reader's terms before using them; answer the question the reader is about to ask; show every part of the data or output the reader will handle; spell out the reasoning behind every choice (numbers of samples and PCRs, factor counts, priors, which function or package demonstrates what); and give practical advice with complete examples, including what the reader should do differently on their own survey. Then a separate copyedit pass: split long sentences, replace "and/or", fix stops, remove asides that repeat their sentence, check every factual claim against the output. The two passes are two commits per lesson so both are visible in review.

- **New run chunks are in scope** when they show data the reader will handle or read an output the prose discusses: a `head()` of each loaded object, a count of each kind of detection error, a cross-tabulation of presence by detection. They are small, run at knit, and use only the bundle.

Reason: with two samples, Lesson 1's field-stage false-positive explanation did not hold (of the 44 field-stage false-positive samples, the other sample at the site was negative for only 17, at a mean fitted site probability of 58.0%; it was contaminated too for 10, at 95.8%, and had a laboratory false positive for 17, at 60.6%), and the first goal of the lessons is to demonstrate how occJSDM works successfully. ### Lesson 1 (report: `style-diagnostic/lesson-1.md`)

- Show the data: `head()` of `survey_data$info`, `$OTU` and `$traits`, and a one-line overview of the `lesson` object, immediately after loading.
- State the two main findings in prose where their outputs appear: occupancy estimates pulled towards the middle (low band 6.5% true against 18.3% estimated in the three-sample rebuild), and the asymmetry between laboratory and field-stage false positives in the all-positive-samples table. Explain the second as a limit of field replication, not as the model believing contamination: a field-stage false positive is a sample that genuinely contains the species' DNA, so at the sample level it is indistinguishable from a true positive and the model rightly gives it a high DNA-presence probability (97.2% on average over the 90 such samples in the default-prior fit); the only evidence against site presence is the other field samples at that site. With three samples per site, the default-prior fit gives those 90 samples a mean site probability of 47.4% (`vignettes/teaching-data/nonspatial-lesson.rds`: `lesson$samples` for the default arm joined to per-sample positives from `lesson$observations`). By what the site's other samples show: 38 with every other sample negative, mean 44.4%; 42 where the other samples carry only laboratory false positives, 38.7%; 6 with another sample also contaminated, 96.1%; 4 with one of each, 94.7%. So a lone contaminated sample beside negative samples leaves the site at a substantial probability on average, but the 38 site probabilities range from 0.7% to 89.7%. The model weighs the contaminated sample's DNA and the negatives beside it with three inputs: the fitted occupancy probability at the site (`occupancy_estimate` in `lesson$samples`, how suitable the habitat is from the environment and the estimated hidden site conditions), the collection probability (the fitted collection probability of the 76 negative samples beside the 38 lone contaminated ones averages 59.1%) and the contamination prior (mean 4.8%; the simulated contamination rates of 2 to 8% sit inside the good-practice assumption). The DNA and the negatives roughly cancel, so the site probability falls back near the fitted occupancy probability: that averages 37.7% for these 38 species and sites, and each site probability is on average 12.4 percentage points from it. A contaminated sample in poor habitat is rejected and one in good habitat is not: the lone sample at the site with the lowest fitted occupancy (0.3%) leaves 0.7%, the one with the highest (82.1%) leaves 89.7%, and Lesson 1's selected field-stage case (OTU_10 at Site 1, fitted occupancy 1.9%) gets 0.7%. Lesson 1 computes this breakdown inline in its `field-stage-false-positive-context` chunk. Laboratory false positives, by contrast, are caught by PCR replication within the sample (781 samples, mean fitted DNA probability 1.4%). The practical advice that follows: more field samples per site, not more PCRs, is what guards site occupancy against contamination at collection. The stress-test section then makes its own, separate point: loosening the contamination priors does not resolve field-stage false positives either (their mean site probability is 41.4% under the alternative priors), because the ambiguity is in the data, not the prior.
- Move convergence ("Are the calculations stable enough to interpret?") before the results, as the quickstart does.

- Collapse the threshold-conversion explanation to one sentence and a link to Lesson 0.

- Keep the unequal-replication section as a demonstration of about 40 lines (what to remove, what the fit needs, that it runs), and move its full truth comparison and second diagnostic system to the appendix.
- Move provenance details and the "Paper2Agent" and "beta-release error target" disclaimers to the appendix or remove them; no reader of the lesson will recognise them. Doug: remove them
- Prior advice (line 432): recommend a basis for changing the contamination priors, namely contamination rates seen in blanks and negative controls, phrased as a suggestion (needs Alex's endorsement before it is stated as package advice).
- Choosing `n_factors` and `n_lattrait` on real data: say plainly that there is no rule yet, start small, and check that conclusions do not change when the count changes.
- Explain why the lesson uses three samples, two primers and six PCRs while the quickstart's `sampledata` uses three, three and two. ### The intuition lesson (new; decided 2 October 2026)

- **Purpose.** A lesson on what occupancy models and JSDMs do, before any fitting, so that Lessons 1 and 3 can refer back to it instead of each re-explaining. Doug's note in "The reader" applies: the explanations remind an ecologist of the ideas rather than introducing them from nothing.
- **Occupancy half.** Start with a one-stage, static occupancy model: sites, replicate samples per site, false negatives only, no false positives. Use the Lesson 0 survey rather than a made-up table: take one species, collapse each sample's PCRs to a sample-level detection, and do the arithmetic by hand in a few lines of R, with no package fit at knit time, because the hand calculation makes the logic visible. The package does fit this classical one-stage occupancy model when `info` has repeated rows per site but no repeated sample identifiers (it reports "occJSDM has inferred occupancy data"), so the half closes with a not-run chunk showing that call on the same one-stage table, and says the detection probability it estimates is the per-sample one the hand calculation found. Teach in order: naive occupancy (the fraction of sites with at least one detection); detectability per sample, estimated from the pattern of detections across replicate samples; the number and proportion of all-negative sites that are nonetheless inferred to be occupied, with the conditional probability psi(1-p)\^M over the total given its own moment, since it is the idea readers struggle with; then how psi covariates (suitable habitat raises the prior) and theta covariates (low collection effort explains the misses) tell us which all-negative sites hold the missing detections; and the design question readers ask, how many replicates until the chance of missing a present species is small, 1-(1-p)\^M. Close by saying false positives are excluded here and arrive in Lesson 1 with the second stage, where the same replication logic protects site occupancy against contamination at collection.
- **JSDM half.** Move the viewer-by-film analogy and the three "what a JSDM adds" points from Lesson 4 section 3 here, and precede them with one toy factorisation of a small sites-by-species presence table with two hidden gradients, so the reader sees the latent axes group sites and species before being told what they mean. Then say what the package's hidden factors, loadings and residual correlations are in those terms, so Lesson 3's ordination and association sections can point back. Source material: Doug's original viewer-by-film draft and a matrix-factorisation worked example are in the sjSDM fork's tutorial, `Code/sjSDM_mojo_tutorial.Rmd` at release v0.2.1 of `dougwyu/s-jSDM`; read it when writing this lesson, though the lesson uses no Mojo.
- **Form.** Every teaching chunk runs at knit from the Lesson 0 bundle or from small inline data; no model fit; figures rendered as for the other lessons; a short closing section naming what the reader can now recognise in Lessons 1 and 3; an appendix only if the hand calculations need a longer derivation.
- **Dependencies.** Written in Phase A after the Lesson 1 pass, so its vocabulary matches Lesson 1's; its number, title, `VignetteIndexEntry`, site-list entry and the back-references from Lessons 1, 3 and 4 are set in the Phase B renumbering. Lesson 0's short two-stage passage and Lesson 1's explanation of the two stages are written to lead into it rather than repeat it.
