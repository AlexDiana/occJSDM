# Mirror-frequency check: results

Run on 13 and 14 October 2026 under the frozen protocol in [README.md](README.md) (commit `c30e90d`); no amendment was needed. The library is `main` at `2748331` under R 4.6.1, installed from a git archive export. Stage a (25 repeats of the pr11 long fit, four chains in one process, 6,000 burn-in and 12,000 retained iterations) ran from 23:45 to 00:33, four repeats at once, 4.5 to 7.7 minutes per repeat. Its decision rule said to run stage b (24,000 burn-in, the same 12,000 retained), which ran from 00:37 to 01:49, 7.1 to 10.9 minutes per repeat. Every repeat completed, and both analyses ran as scripted; the chain exited 0. The tables are under `results/stage-a/` and `results/stage-b/`; the 50 fits (about 14 GB) stay in the archive `dev/simstudy/results/mirror-frequency-20261013`.

## Answer

- **About one chain in thirty settles in the mirror labelling.** Stage a: 4 of 100 chains, share 0.04 (95% Clopper-Pearson interval 0.011 to 0.099), in 3 of the 25 repeats. Stage b: 3 of 100, share 0.03 (0.006 to 0.085), in 3 repeats. Pooled: 7 of 200, share 0.035 (0.014 to 0.071). One further stage-a chain visited both regions (below). No chain was in an unknown region: the largest share of a chain's draws far from both anchors was 2.9%, so the posterior under `main` still matches the two labellings the anchors describe.
- **A longer burn-in does not change it.** Stage b's share lies inside stage a's interval, which is the protocol's rule for "no change" (Fisher's exact test on 4 of 100 against 3 of 100 gives p = 1). The three stage-b mirror chains held the labelling through 24,000 burn-in and 12,000 retained iterations, so the mirror is a stable labelling that a chain enters from the common start, not a transient of the early iterations.
- **Position in the process does not explain it.** Pooled over both stages the mirror counts by chain position are 3, 1, 2 and 1. All three stage-b mirror chains were the first chain of their repeat, which runs from the fresh seed exactly as a separate-process chain does, and no stage-a mirror chain was first. So continuing another chain's random-number stream, the difference the diagnosis could not test, is not what sends a chain to the mirror. The diagnosis's 0 of 16 separate-process chains is what a 3.5% rate predicts most often (probability 0.57), and the pr11 fit's 2 of 4 was an unusual draw (probability of at least 2 of 4 at that rate 0.007).
- **The mirror chains are the labelling the diagnosis described.** Species 6's chain means: field false-positive rate `theta0` 0.250 to 0.255 (truth 0.038; near-truth chains 0.031 to 0.040), first occupancy slope -1.21 to -1.31 (near-truth +0.53 to +0.76), collection intercept -2.77 to -2.83 (near-truth -0.71 to -0.60), while the mean occupancy over the original sites hardly differs (0.443 to 0.462 against 0.454 to 0.497). Every mirror chain had at least 99.99% of its retained draws labelled mirror.
- **The package's own convergence flag catches every repeat with a mirror chain.** In those repeats the rank-normalised Rhat over the four chains is 1.53 to 1.73 for species 6's `theta0`, first slope and collection intercept, far above the 1.1 the package warns at, against at most 1.011 for those quantities in the 43 repeats whose chains all stayed near-truth (and at most 1.017 for `B0`). A user who heeds the warning on this fit sees the split; a user who pools the chains regardless averages two labellings, as the pr11 fit did.
- **One chain moved between the labellings.** Stage-a repeat 25, chain 3, has 13.2% of its retained draws labelled mirror: its first retained draw is in the mirror, it left and returned eleven times, and it left for good after draw 10,471 of 12,000. Its repeat's Rhat was 1.03 to 1.04, under the package's threshold, so this chain would not have been flagged. It shows the mirror is not absorbing, but a chain that reaches it usually stays for the whole run: the other seven never left.

## What this settles, and what it does not

- The TODO item's question is answered: the rate under the current package is about 3 to 4% per chain for this fit, a longer burn-in does not lower it, and stream position does not raise it. Why a chain goes one way or the other from the common start is still not known; it is a property of the first iterations from those starting values under heavy contamination.
- Dispersed starting values were not tested: `runOccJSDM()` has no argument for them, so that test needs a research-only copy of the fitter, as README.md says.
- This is one community of one design (`design-qfar_K6-sites300-05`, high contamination, 300 sites), the only fit in which the mirror has been seen. The rate is for this fit. Doug's 8 October ruling treats high contamination as a deliberate prior-mismatch stress test, and nothing here changes that.
- Timings were taken on a working laptop while the Hmsc library was being installed and the Hmsc check was being built, so they are indicative only.
