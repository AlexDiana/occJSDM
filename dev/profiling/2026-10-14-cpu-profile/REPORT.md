# CPU profile of the sampler: where the time goes and which optimisations are worth doing

Measured on 14 October 2026 against the parallel-chains branch of PR #31 (commit `2b5fe6d`), whose `R/`, `src/` and sampler are what `main` will have once the PR merges. This is the TODO item "Profile current code before choosing further CPU optimizations". The question is which parts of one MCMC iteration cost what, on a representative two-stage fit and a representative binary spatial fit, so that the optimisation candidates in TODO can be ranked by measured share rather than by guesswork.

The one-sentence answer: in both fits the time is in the collapsed spatial-range step, and specifically in the weighted Gram matrices it forms for every species at every point of the range grid, so the candidates that touch that step (an optimised BLAS, the symmetric product, the fused coefficient draw) are worth doing, and the three candidates named in TODO (an alternative Polya-Gamma sampler, reuse of `computePsiCoef()` parts, fusing the `w_all`/`c_imk` work) are not, because together they are under 5 percent of a two-stage iteration and under 0.2 percent of a spatial one.

## What was measured

- The two-stage fit is the quickstart call on the shipped `sampledata`: 100 sites, 300 samples, 1,800 PCR rows, 10 species, two occupancy covariates, two collection covariates, two spatial coordinates, two latent factors, the default 20 support points (a fifth of the sites) and the fixed 10-point range grid. One chain of 1,000 burn-in and 1,000 kept iterations.
- The binary spatial fit is a one-stage binary dataset simulated by `make-binary900.R` with `simulateOccJSDMData()`: 900 sites, 12 species, two covariates, two factors, a spatial field of range 0.08 and unit variance, fitted with the same two covariates and spatial coordinates, two factors and the default 180 support points. One chain of 10 burn-in and 10 kept iterations for the profile, 8 and 8 for the timings. This is the size the shelved stage 2 spatial comparison needs (its `SHELVED.md` asked for exactly this profile).
- Tools: R's `Rprof()` at a 5 ms interval, which attributes time to R functions and to each exported C++ function through its wrapper; macOS `sample` on the running process for 40 s, which attributes time to native functions (BLAS routines, Armadillo, R internals); microbenchmarks of the Gram product at the four sizes that matter; and a prototype of the cheapest change, installed into the loaded namespace and timed against the unchanged code.
- Conditions: Apple M4 laptop (four performance and six efficiency cores), macOS 27.0.1, R 4.6.1 with its reference BLAS (`libRblas.0.dylib`, the one the PR's benchmark declared), `RCPP_PARALLEL_NUM_THREADS` set to 1, one chain in the parent process (`ncores` 1), the package loaded with `pkgload::load_all()` from the worktree. The scripts, logs and summaries are beside this report; `evidence/` holds the Rprof tables, the native-stack sample and the timing logs.

Two limits on the absolute numbers, neither of which affects the shares or the relative gains. First, the laptop was on battery with Low Power Mode on throughout (checked with `pmset` at the end), so every absolute time here is about twice the 13 October benchmark's, which ran on mains: that benchmark's serial chains took 7.9 to 8.1 ms per iteration on the same quickstart fit, against 15.9 ms here from the installed build. Second, a development load is slower than an installed build because the installed package is byte-compiled: on the quickstart fit, 500 plus 500 iterations, the installed export of the same commit took 15.9 ms per iteration and the `load_all()` namespace 18.7 ms (`compare-load.R`), so the R-level shares below are a little inflated relative to what a user sees. Run-to-run variation on this machine is also large: two baseline runs of each fit differ by 6 to 17 percent (`evidence/timings.txt`).

## Where the time goes

### The two-stage quickstart fit

Per-iteration cost: 20.3 and 23.7 ms in the two baseline runs (chain elapsed over 2,000 iterations). Rprof sampled 28.2 s of the first run. Shares of sampled time, by function (`evidence/quickstart-rprof.txt`):

- The JSDM coefficient update, `update_jSDMcoef()`, 72 percent, of which:
  - `spatial_range_logweights()` 53 percent. Inside it: `crossprod()` 30 percent (the weighted Gram matrix `Z' diag(omega) Z` for each of 10 grid points times 10 species, 100 products of a 100 by 25 matrix per iteration, 41 microseconds each), `chol()` 5 percent, `forwardsolve()` 5 percent, `diag()` 4 percent (the prior precision matrix rebuilt 100 times per iteration), and `as.matrix()`, `t()`, `cbind()`, `matrix()` about 8 percent between them.
  - `sample_BBsL_cpp()` 6 percent (it forms the same Gram matrices again, for the chosen grid point only, then draws).
  - `samplePGvariables_parallel()` 2.5 percent, `sample_U_cpp()` 2.2 percent, `sample_GC()` plus `sample_A()` 2.8 percent, `computeVariancePartitioning_stdevs()` 1.9 percent, `computePsiCoef()` 1.2 percent for its three calls per iteration.
- The detection samplers, 17 percent: `sample_betatheta_cpp_parallel()` 6.1, `sample_w_cim_cipp_parallel()` 4.8, `sample_pq_cpp_parallel()` 4.4, `sample_z_cpp_parallel()` 0.9, `sample_theta0()` 0.8.
- The WAIC accumulators, about 5 percent: the second-stage log-likelihood 3.1, `update_waic_summary()` 1.2, the rest small.
- Post-processing after the chain (`computeDiagnostics()`), 1.7 percent, a fixed cost per fit.
- The `w_all`/`c_imk` lines (the `[`, `*`, `-` and `==` entries together) about 1 percent.

### The binary spatial fit at 900 sites

Per-iteration cost: 6.6 and 7.0 s in the two baseline runs (chain elapsed over 16 iterations), so a 10,000-iteration chain would take about 18 hours under these conditions, or roughly 9 on mains. Rprof sampled 84.6 s of the 20-iteration profile (`evidence/binary900-rprof.txt`):

- `spatial_range_logweights()` 87.6 percent, of which `crossprod()` alone is 83.6 percent: 120 weighted Gram products of a 900 by 185 matrix per iteration, 47 ms each with the reference BLAS.
- `sample_BBsL_cpp()` 9.0 percent: the same product 12 times more, for the chosen grid point, inside `XtOmegaX_SoR()`.
- `chol()` 1.5 percent, `computeDiagnostics()` 2.1 percent (fixed per fit, large here only because the chain is short), `precomputeSORmatrices()` 0.7 percent (fixed per fit), everything else under 0.2 percent each, including the Polya-Gamma draws at 0.08 percent.

The native stacks say the same thing from below (`evidence/binary900-native-sample.txt`): of the samples on the main thread, 95 percent have `dgemm_` from `libRblas.0.dylib` at the top of the stack; `dsyrk_`, `dtrsm_` and the garbage collector are under 1 percent each. The cost scales with the number of sites times the square of the number of support points, times the grid length, times the species count.

## The BLAS is the bottleneck, and Doug's suggestion to switch to Accelerate is the largest lever available

R's reference BLAS runs the 900 by 185 Gram product at about 1.3 GFLOPS. Apple's Accelerate framework (vecLib), which the CRAN build of R ships as `libRblas.vecLib.dylib` beside the reference library, runs the same product in 0.17 ms instead of 47 ms (`acc-gram.c`, `evidence/gram-microbenchmarks.txt`), single-threaded, because it uses the M4's matrix units. The same program gives 0.005 ms against 0.083 ms at the quickstart's 100 by 25 size, where call overhead dominates. Because both R's `crossprod()` and the package's Armadillo code link `libRblas.dylib` through the symlink (checked with `otool -L` on `occJSDM.so`), and R's LAPACK calls the same BLAS inside `chol()`, switching the symlink accelerates every matrix product in the sampler at once, with no package change.

- Expected effect, estimated from the shares rather than measured end to end: at 900 sites the Gram products are 95 percent of the iteration and would shrink roughly 200-fold, so the iteration would fall from about 6.6 s to about 0.3 s, around 20 times faster, with the remaining time in R-level overhead, the Cholesky factorisations and the samplers. At the quickstart size the products are about 30 percent of the iteration and would shrink 10 to 15-fold, so about 1.4 times faster. The shelved stage 2 spatial comparison, which was shelved because of exactly this cost, may become feasible on the laptop.
- The switch could not be measured end to end here: `DYLD_LIBRARY_PATH` is ignored by the signed R binary (tried; `extSoftVersion()` still named the reference library), and the symlink lives in the root-owned R framework. The R for macOS FAQ (section "Which BLAS is used and how can it be changed?") gives the switch as two commands, run as an administrator; `sessionInfo()` then names `libRblas.vecLib.dylib`, and `sh timing-runs.sh <run-dir>` from this directory measures the fits.

```sh
cd /Library/Frameworks/R.framework/Resources/lib
sudo ln -sf libRblas.vecLib.dylib libRblas.dylib   # Accelerate
sudo ln -sf libRblas.0.dylib libRblas.dylib        # back to the reference BLAS
```

- Caveats, so the decision is informed. The FAQ keeps the reference BLAS as the default "for precision" and warns that vecLib "is not under our control and may possibly deliver inaccurate results"; the package's products are well-conditioned Cholesky and Gram operations, so this is a reason to compare a vecLib fit with a reference fit once (posterior summaries, not bits), not a reason to avoid the switch. Results under vecLib differ from the reference BLAS at rounding level, so a fit is reproducible under one BLAS but not across the two: PR #31's identity tests are gated on `OCCJSDM_REFERENCE_BLAS`, and under vecLib that declaration must not be made and those tests are skipped, while the RNG-restoration tests still run. Accelerate is threaded by default; the PR already sets `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS` to 1 for its workers, and the serial path should do the same when results must be reproducible, since a thread count can change rounding. The CRAN installer resets the symlink on every R upgrade (the PR's BLAS evidence records the symlink's 7 October modification time, the install date), so the switch must be repeated after upgrades. It is macOS-only; on a Linux server the equivalent is building R against OpenBLAS or MKL, which the stage 2 study should ask for.
- A package-side alternative would be to link Accelerate directly from `src/` for the Gram products on macOS; that would not depend on the user's R configuration but adds platform-specific build logic and is not the first step. Do the symlink first and measure.

## Package changes, ranked by measured or prototyped share

The ranking assumes the reference BLAS, which is what users get by default; under an optimised BLAS the BLAS-bound items shrink and the overhead items (3 and 4) grow in relative importance.

1. **Form the Gram matrices with the symmetric routine.** `crossprod(Z, w * Z)` calls `dgemm`; `crossprod(sqrt(w) * Z)` calls `dsyrk` and halves the flops (47 to 23 ms at 900 by 185, 0.083 to 0.051 ms at 100 by 25). Armadillo dispatches `Zw.t() * Zw` to `dsyrk` as well (23.5 ms, the same as an explicit call), so `XtOmegaX_SoR()` gets the same halving by scaling the design by `sqrt(Omega)` before the product. `proto-logweights.R` is a working prototype of the R side of this, with the prior precision matrix, `sqrt(Omega)` and the fixed design block hoisted out of the loops, `backsolve(C, h, transpose = TRUE)` in place of `forwardsolve(t(C), h)`, and no scatter into `H` when the basis is full (which it always is for new fits). Its log-weights agree with the current code's to a relative 4e-16 on every checked call (the first three calls of each run are compared, largest absolute difference 1.8e-12, same argmax every time). Measured against the unchanged code, back to back: at 900 sites 6.6 to 4.9 s and 7.0 to 4.8 s per iteration (1.35 to 1.45 times); at the quickstart 20.3 to 18.3 ms and 23.7 to 18.1 ms (1.1 to 1.3 times). The profile of the prototype shows why the 900-site gain is less than two: `crossprod()` falls to 78 percent of the sampled time (26 ms per product, as the microbenchmark predicts) and `sample_BBsL_cpp()`, unchanged in the prototype, rises to 12 percent. The change is not bit-identical to current fits, as no change to this step can be, so it needs the same treatment as any sampler change: a refit comparison and new reference numbers. Effort: small. Gain under the reference BLAS: up to 1.5 times at spatial sizes once the C++ side is included.
2. **Reuse the chosen grid point's factorisation for the coefficient draw.** `spatial_range_logweights()` already forms, for every grid point and species, exactly the precision matrix `Q = Z' diag(omega) Z + P` and the linear term `h` that `sample_BBsL_cpp()` then rebuilds for the chosen grid point (`XtOmegaX_SoR()` and `XtK_SoR()` compute the same quantities with the same prior layout; checked line by line). The draw is `backsolve(C, v + z)` with `C = chol(Q)`, `v = C^-T h` and `z` standard normal, both of which the log-weight step already has. Keeping `C` and `v` for the chosen grid point (or for all grid points: 10 by 12 by 185 by 185 doubles is 33 MB at 900 sites) removes the Gram and Cholesky work from `sample_BBsL_cpp()`: 9 to 12 percent of a spatial iteration, 6 percent of a quickstart iteration. The draw must stay in C++ on the package's generator, consuming the same count of normals in the same species order, so that PR #31's per-chain seeding contract is unchanged; only the arithmetic path changes. Effort: moderate, because the live serial `sample_BBsL_cpp()` path is the one TODO says to preserve until a validated replacement exists, and this is that replacement. Gain: about 1.1 times on top of item 1.
3. **Move the range step's loops to C++.** Under the reference BLAS the R-level overhead of `spatial_range_logweights()` (the allocations, `diag()`, `as.matrix()`, `t()`, `cbind()`, the `vapply()` closures) is about 20 percent of a quickstart iteration and nothing at 900 sites. Under an optimised BLAS it becomes the dominant cost of the step at every size, because the products vanish and the per-call allocation of a 900 by 185 design and its scaled copy for each of 120 species-grid pairs (more than 150 MB of allocation per iteration) remains. An Armadillo implementation with items 1 and 2 built in would remove most of it. Effort: moderate. Gain: 1.2 times at the quickstart under the reference BLAS; the main remaining gain once the BLAS is fast.
4. **Thread the range step across species or grid points.** The 120 Gram products per iteration are independent and involve no random draws, so `RcppParallel::parallelFor` over them is safe under the sampling contract (no RNG on worker threads) and, with a serial BLAS, bit-identical across thread counts, which keeps PR #31's identity property. On this laptop four chains already occupy the four performance cores, so there is nothing to gain here; on a server with spare cores it scales the dominant step. Only after item 3, since it needs the C++ implementation. Effort: small once item 3 exists.
5. **The detection samplers, next tier for two-stage fits (17 percent together).** `sample_pq_cpp_parallel()` (4.4 percent) runs `maxP` passes over all PCR rows per species, each testing four compound conditions; one pass accumulating the four counts per primer gives the same integers and the same draws. `sample_betatheta_cpp_parallel()` (6.1 percent) is per-species Polya-Gamma regressions on the rows with `z = 1`; `sample_w_cim_cipp_parallel()` (4.8 percent) is the per-row probability loop. Each is a few percent; none is worth touching before items 1 to 3.
6. **Fewer range-grid evaluations per iteration (a sampler change, Alex's call).** The exact categorical update evaluates all 10 grid points every iteration, which is why the range step costs 10 times the coefficient draw. A Metropolis move on the grid index with a neighbour proposal evaluates 2 points per iteration, a fivefold reduction of the dominant cost, at the price of slower mixing across the grid and a new validation of the kind in `dev/simstudy/spatial-range-validation.md`, which was written for the exact update. Flagged, not recommended, because items 1 to 3 and the BLAS switch get more without changing the kernel.

## Candidates that the profile rules out for now

- **An alternative Polya-Gamma sampler.** `samplePGvariables_parallel()` is 2.5 percent of a quickstart iteration (1,000 draws at about 350 ns each) and 0.08 percent of a spatial one; the detection coefficient sampler's own draws are part of its 6.1 percent. A faster sampler could save at most about 4 percent of a two-stage iteration. The current sampler already uses the large-argument normal approximation above 12.
- **Reusing unchanged `computePsiCoef()` components.** Its three calls per iteration are 1.2 percent of a quickstart iteration and 0.1 percent of a spatial one.
- **Fusing the `w_all`/`c_imk` work.** About 1 percent of a quickstart iteration; `y_pos` is already outside the loop.
- **Skipping the fitting-time WAIC.** About 5 percent of a quickstart iteration, nothing of a spatial one. Worth an option for its own reasons, not for speed.
- **Avoiding repeated inversions in the trait and factor updates.** `sample_GC()`, `sample_A()` and `sampleBuniv()` together are 2.8 percent of a quickstart iteration.
- **Vectorised initial values.** Start-up does not appear in either profile at these schedule lengths.
- **Pinning or adding RcppParallel threads.** No sampler in the hot path uses them (the range step is R code and `sample_BBsL_cpp()` is serial), which is why the PR's benchmark saw no difference from thread counts; the only threaded workers are the detection probability loops, under 10 percent of a two-stage iteration.

## How to rerun

From a checkout root with the package compiled (`pkgload::load_all()` must succeed), on mains power with Low Power Mode off:

```sh
OUT=/Users/douglasyu/src/occJSDM/dev/simstudy/results/cpu-profile-$(date +%Y%m%d)
mkdir -p "$OUT"
Rscript dev/profiling/2026-10-14-cpu-profile/make-binary900.R "$OUT/binary900.rds"
Rscript dev/profiling/2026-10-14-cpu-profile/profile-fit.R quickstart 1000 1000 "$OUT/quickstart"
Rscript dev/profiling/2026-10-14-cpu-profile/profile-fit.R binary900 10 10 "$OUT/binary900" "$OUT/binary900.rds"
sh dev/profiling/2026-10-14-cpu-profile/timing-runs.sh "$OUT"
```

`profile-fit.R` prints the Rprof tables and saves them as an `.rds`; with `NOPROF=1` it only times the chain; with `PROTO=1` it installs `proto-logweights.R` (copied into the run directory by `timing-runs.sh`) and checks its first three calls against the original. `compare-load.R` times an installed build against the development load. `acc-gram.c` is the Accelerate microbenchmark (`clang -O2 -DACCELERATE_NEW_LAPACK -o acc-gram acc-gram.c -framework Accelerate`). To profile native stacks, start a fit and run `sample <pid> 40 -file out.txt` on the R process.
