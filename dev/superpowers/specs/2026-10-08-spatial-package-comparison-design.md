## Purpose and boundaries

Find a useful way to estimate spatial effects in occJSDM by comparing spatial representations on the same known landscapes. Extend Lesson 5 with occJSDM, sjSDM, Hmsc and gllvm, emphasising occJSDM's existing GP, spatial basis covariates and sjSDM's coordinate neural network. Link the results from Lesson 7. Success is reliable recovery and prediction with honest uncertainty and diagnostics; a visually detailed map is not sufficient.

The first experiment uses only the site-arrangement sweep's perfectly observed binary arm. It retains all three communities, four arrangements, eight species and 1,600 unobserved lattice locations per community. There is no new simulation, selection of favourable communities, detection-model comparison, production sampler change, prior-default change or website publication. Doug explicitly deferred spOccupancy's `sfJSDM()`. Record a brief literature-based capability review of boral, jSDM and GJAM, but do not fit extra packages. Hmsc and gllvm's spatial factors remain in scope.

The final recommendation can be that no tested model recovers enough spatial information at this sampling budget. This study cannot establish that 5% species are universally unidentifiable, that the oracle is an unbeatable bound in every realised dataset, or that a spatial association identifies dispersal or another ecological process.

## Hypotheses and what the comparisons identify

1. A fixed spatial basis may recover more useful structure than the existing GP at 100 sites because it changes both the spatial representation and its coefficient prior. In occJSDM these coefficients still share the occupancy-coefficient hierarchy, including `sigma_b`; they do not acquire independent per-species shrinkage merely by entering `occCovariates`.
2. sjSDM's DNN may learn spatial features that a quadratic surface cannot represent. Its feature map is learned, not a fixed basis. Elastic-net regularisation replaces neither the need for information nor the need to check overfitting.
3. Hmsc and gllvm's shared spatial factors may reduce estimation noise, but a two-factor representation is deliberately restrictive relative to the eight independently generated species fields. Report this model mismatch rather than assuming that low rank is correct.
4. Strong apparent spatial contributions for rare species may fail to improve true-probability error or held-out prediction. These landscapes all have nonzero fields, so the experiment does not estimate a formal false-positive rate for detecting a spatial effect under a zero-field null.

Changing package changes more than a sampler: links, priors, covariance assumptions and optimisation differ. Within-package comparisons hold the non-spatial configuration fixed wherever possible. Across packages, common predictors establish matched information, not identical statistical models. Preserve the historical GP result separately from any diagnostic-selected extension.

## Spatial bases and new-site transformations

Keep raw unit-square coordinates for distance calculations. Standardise environmental columns using training means and sample SDs only. For the quadratic trend, first standardise x and y using training sites, form `x`, `y`, `x^2`, `x*y`, `y^2`, then centre and scale these five columns from training sites. Save both transformations and apply them unchanged at prediction sites. Keep the intercept separate. Do not claim the chosen penalty is invariant to basis scaling.

Use a reproducible Moran-eigenvector construction for the `mem` arm. Build a symmetric binary adjacency W from the union of directed eight-nearest-neighbour edges between training sites, excluding self edges; resolve distance ties by stable site ID. Let H centre the training sites and decompose `H W H`. Retain the ten largest positive eigenvalues, using a positivity tolerance of `1e-10 * max(1, max(abs(eigenvalues)))`; if fewer exist, retain all positive ones and record the realised dimension; if none exist, mark the basis unavailable and preserve the cell. Resolve eigenvector signs deterministically. Never select eigenvectors using responses or truth. This is a specified Moran basis inspired by the literature, not an assertion that it reproduces Leibold's exact neighbourhood construction.

Predict the same basis with a Nyström extension. For a new point, connect to its eight nearest training sites and to any training site for which its distance is within that site's saved eighth-neighbour radius. Use the union, with the same tie rule. At an exactly matching training coordinate, return its stored training row. If w is the resulting cross-adjacency row, define `c = w - mean(w) - colMeans(W) + mean(W)` and extend eigenvector v with eigenvalue d as `c %*% v / d`. Apply the stored training eigenvector centring/scaling afterwards. Do not diagonalise a new matrix containing test sites. Record disconnected graph components and basis amplitudes at distant prediction sites; do not repair them using the test outcomes. Basis failure stops that arm and remains visible.

The DNN receives training-standardised coordinates. The native Hmsc/gllvm spatial arms receive raw coordinates so their distance units are explicit; freeze and record Hmsc's realised default range grid and gllvm's range parameterisation. No graph neighbourhood, range grid, number of factors or penalty is tuned with the known generating range or lattice truth. Native occJSDM retains its historical internal axis-wise standardisation; audit that transformation separately.

## Prediction and recovery targets

The common target is the generating occurrence probability at a particular site on the realised landscape, conditioned on training observations, environment and location. It is not a new independent landscape, a prediction conditioned on held-out species responses, or the probability after setting uncertain spatial effects to their posterior mean. For Bayesian/variational spatial predictions integrate the inverse link over the appropriate predictive latent-field uncertainty, then over available parameter uncertainty. Native uncertainty omissions must be labelled. Use the 1,600 lattice probabilities for scoring; do not generate new observations because the archive already contains lattice binary states for optional proper scoring rules.

Preserve the distinction between posterior uncertainty in occurrence probability and Bernoulli uncertainty in a future state. For sjSDM, verify the effective marginal probit scale from the frozen implementation, including its fixed diagonal residual term. A direct inverse link of an unnormalised neural-network output is not a certified occurrence probability. For gllvm, a default call with `newX` and no `newLV` omits latent variables; it must not be labelled a spatial prediction.

Resolve the arbitrary spatial intercept consistently. For each species and draw, subtract the mean fitted spatial contribution across the 100 training sites and add that mean to the environmental/intercept contribution. Carry the same training centring to the lattice. Centre the true field identically. For occJSDM GP prediction, preserve the fitted subset-of-regressors model: coefficient uncertainty is available, but full-GP residual conditional variance outside its support is not automatically part of that fitted model. Do not add such variance while labelling it native prediction. Full probabilities must be unchanged to numerical tolerance. Define the comparable spatial effect as `delta_p = p_full - p_space_zero`, keeping the environmental/intercept term and any independent residual integration fixed. Average probability differences over draws. Compute the same contrast from the true model. Native logit-field recovery supplements this probability-scale target; never put logit truth on an unconverted probit-amplitude axis or use a universal logit/probit multiplier.

Report the following, per species, arrangement and community, then by the 5%, 25% and 75% groups. Weight communities equally and keep paired contrasts within community and arrangement. Show all three community results and their range; do not treat sites or species as independent replicate communities.

1. Spatial-pattern recovery: centred field RMSE, correlation and attenuation slope against the zero-field baseline and oracle for compatible logit fields; for every package, error and correlation of `delta_p` against its truth. A constant estimated field has correlation recorded as unavailable, not zero or a silently dropped row.
2. Amplitude recovery: realised spatial SD and attenuation on matching link scales, and RMS probability contrast across packages. Distinguish the realised SD on sampled sites from the generating GP SD of 1. Report native hyperparameters separately.
3. Occurrence probability: signed error, MAE and RMSE at training and lattice sites, stratified by prevalence group and true probability below 0.2, from 0.2 through 0.8, and above 0.8. Retain all-zero and all-one species.
4. Held-out performance: lattice errors overall and by nearest-training-site distance in bins up to 0.02, above 0.02 through 0.05, above 0.05 through 0.1 and above 0.1; compare full prediction, zeroed spatial contribution, and the separately fitted `env` arm. Brier and log scores against saved lattice states are secondary. Keep empty bins with count zero and unavailable scores.
5. Uncertainty and diagnostics: available 95% probability/field intervals, widths and descriptive truth inclusion, with their target and conditioning stated; full convergence/optimisation records, runtime and failures. Coverage over three landscapes is descriptive, not evidence of universal calibration.

Reproduce the archived oracle and GP field scores using their historical pointwise-median-then-centre convention before introducing the common probability-scale scores. Keep both tables and explain their different targets. The oracle knows the true coefficients, kernel and amplitude and therefore supplies a useful favourable reference. It is not a hard finite-sample upper bound for every correlation, median-based loss or individual community. Negative gaps to the oracle are retained and investigated rather than clipped.

Compare range parameters only when the covariance functions, distance metrics and link-scale interpretation genuinely match. Otherwise show each package's kernel definition and an explicitly descriptive distance to a fixed correlation, such as 0.5. An exponential spatial factor has no unique squared-exponential generating range to recover. Trend, MEM and DNN arms have no fitted GP range parameter. Do not score network weights or basis coefficients against GP kernel parameters.

## Per-site attribution and rare species

Implement study-only component-ablation attribution, not a new exported occJSDM variance-partition API. For the selected model, centre both environmental linear-predictor contributions and spatial contributions over training sites and absorb both means into the intercept. Form four marginal probability predictions, retaining the same draw/parameter distribution: intercept only, environment, space, and environment plus space. Let their summed Bernoulli log scores across species at a site be `L0`, `LE`, `LS`, `LES`. Attribute environmental gain as `0.5 * ((LE - L0) + (LES - LS))` and spatial gain as `0.5 * ((LS - L0) + (LES - LE))`. Their sum must equal `LES - L0`. This is a symmetric predictive attribution of a fitted model, not a refitted likelihood-ratio test or a variance decomposition.

Calculate the attribution against observed training states, saved lattice states and expected log score under the known probabilities. Preserve per-species contributions so the 5% group remains visible; preserve negative values and the baseline/total gain. Use a numerical log-score clamp of `1e-12` only while evaluating logarithms and count clamped cells. Do not rescale negative gains into positive ternary shares. Plot inferred spatial contribution against true spatial contrast and held-out benefit. A large training contribution with no truth or lattice improvement is evidence of unhelpful fitted structure in this experiment, not a measured general false-positive rate.

Document that Cai's native sjSDM ANOVA/internal-structure calculation uses different model comparisons and includes biotic covariance. It is not numerically interchangeable with this attribution. The primary controlled arms have no unstructured biotic component, so an E/S/C ternary plot would be misleading. Native partitions may be described in the capability review but are not silently substituted for the common scores.

## Diagnostics, uncertainty and finite fitting budgets

For every new Bayesian arm use four chains, 2,000 warm-up and 4,000 retained draws per chain. Permit one extension to four chains, 8,000 warm-up and 16,000 retained draws when any scored identifiable coefficient, species probability mean, training field element, spatial SD or range function has rank-normalised Rhat above 1.01, bulk/tail ESS below 400, unavailable diagnostics for a nonconstant quantity, or a native convergence warning. Do not screen arbitrary rotated factor coordinates instead of the reconstructed species fields. Zero spatial fields in `env` arms are structural constants and exempt. Retain genuine degenerate sampled chains as diagnostic failures. Recheck archived GP fits with this stricter screen; any new extension supplements and never overwrites their historical record.

gllvm uses three independent residual-based VA starts, at most 12,000 optimiser iterations each. Require a successful native convergence flag, finite maximum absolute gradient below 0.01, and a finite objective. Allow three additional starts if the best acceptable solution is not reproduced within 0.1 objective units and 0.01 probability at every training site/species. Select the best acceptable objective before truth scoring; retain every start. If none is acceptable, the cell is failed; if a best acceptable solution lacks replication at six starts, score it with an unresolved-stability flag.

sjSDM uses three initialisations, 3,000 epochs at learning rate 0.002 followed by 1,000 epochs at 0.0002 with a fresh optimiser, 2,000 Monte Carlo draws, full batches, CPU float64 and weight decay 0.0001. Disable adaptive early stopping and learning-rate scheduling. Include spatial-network parameters in both optimisation phases; the old non-spatial continuation code only includes environmental weights and covariance and must not be copied unchanged. With diagonal covariance, independently evaluate the normalised marginal likelihood and exact implemented penalties. Require finite objectives and a maximum absolute stationarity residual below 0.01: ordinary gradients for smooth parameters and KKT/subgradient residuals at L1 zeros. Record the objective normalisation and the check convention. Use the same objective/prediction agreement and three-plus-three start rule as gllvm. Never select by closeness to truth or lattice predictions. A failed diagnostic at the finite budget is a reported outcome, not permission for unlimited retries.

Bayesian probability/field intervals are computed from draws. gllvm intervals must state which fixed-effect and variational/conditional prediction uncertainty they include, and report Hessian or covariance failures. sjSDM's environmental coefficient standard errors do not establish DNN spatial-probability intervals; mark these as unavailable in the primary study. Do not call a spread across optimisation starts a confidence interval. A bootstrap calibration experiment is outside this first plan.

Maximum main-study attempts: 84 new Bayesian initial runs plus at most 96 Bayesian extensions, and 96 point-fit cells with at most six starts each, giving at most 756 new fitting attempts. This excludes small synthetic adapter checks and includes diagnostic extensions of all 12 reused GP cells. Use at most four concurrent worker processes and one computational thread per worker. Measure runtime on the prespecified first community's clustered and spread cells after adapter certification, retain those fits as study attempts, and publish a remaining-runtime estimate before dispatching the rest. Record a resource interruption separately from model failure; record metadata and any natively supported partial state before stopping an owned worker reaching 24 hours. A partial object is never a selected fit; do not claim sampler checkpoint/resume support unless it has been tested. Leave the affected arm interrupted pending an explicit budget amendment rather than silently retrying it or signalling unrelated processes. Changes to the attempt or resource budget need a dated amendment.

## Verification and completion criteria

Before fitting, test archived identifier/hash integrity, no regeneration fallback, training-only transformations, Moran training-row reproduction, predictor permutations, all-zero species, probit marginal normalisation, conditional Gaussian prediction variance, and array dimensions. Before scoring, verify immutable diagnostic-based selections and original-input identities. Reproduce the historical oracle/GP scores to `1e-10` where computed from the same summaries; compare native/custom probabilities to `1e-6`, and certified Gaussian integration to `1e-6`. Stochastic native checks must report Monte Carlo SE and require a discrepancy within five SE plus `1e-6`, with a finite refinement budget; they cannot claim deterministic precision.

Completion requires all 192 cells accounted for, supported outputs independently verified, unsupported targets explicitly documented, frozen settings and failures preserved, compact figures/data and the two lesson sources reviewed, and a recommendation about which spatial representation merits further occJSDM development. Completion does not require finding a superior model. Planning ends before dependency installation, archive recovery, fitting or lesson edits; those belong to an approved execution turn.
